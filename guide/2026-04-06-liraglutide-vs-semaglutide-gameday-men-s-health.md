What Is Liraglutide?

Liraglutide is a medication classified as a GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, which is a naturally occurring hormone in your body. This hormone is released in response to food intake and plays a major role in regulating appetite, blood sugar, and digestion.

As a GLP-1 receptor agonist, liraglutide mimics the action of this natural hormone. It binds to GLP-1 receptors in the body and activates them, leading to several important effects: (1)

Slowing the rate at which the stomach empties

Increasing feelings of fullness after eating

Reducing appetite and food cravings

Stimulating insulin release when blood sugar rises

Suppressing excess glucagon secretion

Liraglutide was initially developed and approved for the management of type 2 diabetes. Over time, researchers observed that patients taking it experienced meaningful weight loss. (2) This led to its approval at higher doses specifically for chronic weight management in certain individuals.

One important characteristic of liraglutide is its duration of action. It is designed to work over a 24-hour period, which means it is typically administered as a once-daily injection. Depending on how men feel about daily injections, it may not be a great fit, especially for those with busy daily schedules.

What Is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is also a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Like liraglutide, it mimics the action of the body’s natural GLP-1 hormone. However, semaglutide has structural differences that make it more resistant to breakdown in the body. As a result, it typically lasts longer and has a stronger effect at comparable doses than liraglutide.

Mechanistically, semaglutide works in very similar ways such as: (3)

It enhances insulin secretion in response to elevated blood sugar

It reduces glucagon output

It slows gastric emptying

It acts on appetite centers in the brain to reduce hunger

The key distinction lies in its potency and duration. Semaglutide has a much longer half-life, allowing it to remain active in the body for about a week. Because of this, it is typically administered as a once-weekly injection rather than needing to remember daily injections.

Semaglutide was also first used in the management of type 2 diabetes. Like liraglutide, it later gained approval at higher doses for chronic weight management after clinical trials demonstrated significant reductions in body weight. (4)

For many men, the once-weekly dosing schedule is appealing, as it may reduce the burden of remembering daily injections and may feel less disruptive to a busy routine. However, others prefer the daily routine of liraglutide, finding it easier to remember and plan the medication when it’s administered every day.
