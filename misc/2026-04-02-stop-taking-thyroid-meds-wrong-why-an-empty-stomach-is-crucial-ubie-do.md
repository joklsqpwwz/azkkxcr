Why must thyroid medicine be taken on an empty stomach?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

[Taking Thyroid Meds](https://allpepay.com/)

Thyroid medication should be taken on an empty stomach to ensure proper absorption. Food, beverages, and certain supplements can bind to the medication in your digestive tract, reducing how much your body absorbs and making treatment less effective.

Best practices for taking thyroid medicine:

Take it first thing in the morning, at least 30–60 minutes before eating or drinking anything other than water

Taking thyroid medicine on an empty stomach is important because food can interfere with the absorption of the medication. This means that if you take the medicine with food, your body might not get all the benefits it needs from the medication.

Absorption Issues: According to the references, food can affect the levels of thyroid hormones in your blood. This is because the presence of food in the stomach can change how much medicine your body takes in.

Timing: One study suggests that taking thyroid medicine at different times of the day, like morning or evening, can also change how well it works. However, taking it consistently at the same time each day, ideally on an empty stomach, is important.

Liquid Formulation: There is some research on liquid forms of the medicine, which might be taken with meals in certain cases, but this is specific to certain types of formulations and not the usual practice.

Overall, following the advice of taking thyroid medicine on an empty stomach helps ensure that the medication works as effectively as possible.
