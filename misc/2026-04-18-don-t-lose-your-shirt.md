A fraction of an inch often doesn't matter in clothing, except in sleeves. The cuff should reach the wrist bone when your arm is at your side. Too long and you're 14 again, wearing Dad's shirt. Too short and you're a gangly 16, wearing a shirt that fit when you were 14.

When you're not wearing a jacket, ill-fitting sleeves can be rolled up. With a suit, you want a half inch of cuff exposed. As you button a dress shirt, stop somewhere between Tom Jones and Forrest Gump. True, Gump went far with all buttons secured and Tom Jones's exposed chest inspires women to toss their undergarments to him. But neither of these looks works in the real world.

Style guru John Bartlett likes a conservative showing of chest hair and a hint of an undershirt. It depends partly on the buttons' placement, but generally "two buttons open at the neck is the perfect combination of cool and sexy," says Bartlett. "Any more than that and you'll be showing more breast than your date. Any less, you'll look like a missionary."

The white dress shirt is a five-tool player. It throbs with crisp and clean, and can do just about anything. Paired with blue jeans, it shouts wholesome and strong, at once cowboy and cosmopolitan. Under a suit, it sings of honesty. A white dress shirt is a pristine backdrop against which to play with necktie color, a blank slate on which she can write her fantasies. How does white do all that? That's the mystery.

The rules: Don't miss a chance to add some intrigue at your wrist. French cuffs and the cuff links they demand are an instant class transfusion. But most men wear them only at very formal or black-tie events where every loser and his brother will be sporting shine. Break out some glint or some silk knots when nobody else will dare and you'll lift yourself above the drones. This doesn't mean at the PTA meeting; but the office Christmas party will do just fine.

It's equal-opportunity fashion: No matter what your hair color or skin tone, body size or noggin shape, you'll look sharp in crisp white cloth. Employ it to your benefit: with a business suit and power tie to seal the deal, or untucked with jeans or khakis to convey casual elegance on a date.

Invest in at least one well-made, durable white dress shirt--you'll wear it often. "Quality dress shirts have mother-of-pearl or bone buttons, never plastic," says Andy Gilchrist, author of The Encyclopedia of Men's Clothes. "And there should be enough buttons down the front to keep gaps at a minimum."

Look for cotton poplin for a moderately priced shirt, or go higher-end with pima cotton or a silk-cotton blend.

The ascent of the colorful shirt is a frame buster for men's fashion. Italian designer Kean Etro started it years ago, and now designers around the world have declared their independence from blandness. Tommy Hilfiger is betting on vibrant stripes this spring. Nyne is growing its clothing presence with bright, bold stripeys.

"Since the shirt is such a strong statement, your pants and outerwear should be neutral," says Brian Boye'. "Jeans and khakis work well." Paired with a solid suit, colorful shirts are a high-impact blend of business and buoyant.
