# Safe Pregnancy Weight Gain: Vital Doctor Tips You Need Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 5/16/2026

How to Gain Weight Safely During Pregnancy: Tips from Your Doctor

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Healthy weight gain during pregnancy supports your baby's development and builds essential nutritional reserves for breastfeeding. Most women should aim to gain 25 to 35 pounds total, though this target adjusts based on pre-pregnancy weight, with calorie needs increasing gradually across each trimester alongside balanced macronutrient and micronutrient goals.

Key strategies include eating smaller, frequent meals, choosing calorie-dense and nutrient-rich snacks, prioritizing protein and healthy fats, drinking adequate fluids, staying physically active, and tracking your weight consistently.

Explanation

Gaining the right amount of weight during pregnancy supports your baby's growth and helps you stay healthy. Every pregnancy is unique, but most women need to gain between 25 and 35 pounds (11–16 kg). If you were underweight before pregnancy, your doctor might recommend a bit more; if you were overweight, a bit less. Here's how to gain weight during pregnancy in a safe, steady way.

Gaining too little or too much can increase risks. Talk with your healthcare provider about your individual goal.

Calorie and Nutrient Goals

Daily Calorie Increase

First trimester: Focus on nutrient quality more than extra calories (only about 100–150 extra kcal/day).

Second and third trimesters: Aim for an additional 300–450 kcal/day above your pre-pregnancy needs.

Macronutrients

Protein: 70–100 g/day. Lean meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, and soy.

Healthy fats: Include avocados, nuts, seeds, olive oil, and fatty fish (salmon, sardines) for omega-3s.

Carbohydrates: 175 g or more/day. Focus on whole grains, fruits, and vegetables.

Key Micronutrients

Folate/folic acid: 600 μg/day to reduce neural tube defects

Iron: 27 mg/day to prevent anemia

Calcium: 1,000 mg/day for bone health

Vitamin D: 600 IU/day to aid calcium absorption

Iodine: 220 μg/day for baby's brain development

Most prenatal vitamins cover these needs. Always check with your doctor before adding supplements.

Overcoming Common Challenges

Morning Sickness and Food Aversions

Eat dry crackers or toast first thing in the morning.

Sip ginger tea or suck on ginger candies.

Try cold meals (pasta salad, sandwiches) if hot foods smell off.

Heartburn and Indigestion

Avoid laying down right after eating.

Feeling Too Full

Low Appetite

Keep healthy snacks within reach (on your nightstand or desk).

Gentle Exercise to Support Healthy Gain

Physical activity helps manage mood, reduce swelling, and can improve appetite. Aim for 150 minutes per week of moderate exercise, such as:

Brisk walking

Swimming or water aerobics

Prenatal yoga or Pilates

Stationary cycling

Always get your doctor's OK before starting or continuing any workout routine.

When to Reach Out for Medical Advice

Weight gain can vary from person to person. Talk to your healthcare provider if you notice:

Little to no weight gain for more than 2 weeks

Rapid or excessive weight gain (more than 5 lbs/2.3 kg in one week)

Severe or persistent nausea and vomiting

Signs of dehydration (dark urine, dizziness)

New or worsening pain, bleeding, or swelling

Final Thoughts

Gaining weight safely during pregnancy is a balance of nutrition, self-care, and medical guidance. By choosing wholesome foods, managing symptoms, and staying active, you'll support both your health and your baby's development.

Remember: every body is different. Always speak to a doctor about your unique needs, especially if you experience anything that feels serious or life-threatening. They are your best ally in making sure you and your baby stay healthy and strong throughout this incredible journey.
