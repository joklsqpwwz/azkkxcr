The Biology of Aging and Alcohol

Why does your body suddenly struggle to “shrug off” a few drinks? The shift is rooted in several physiological changes that begin as early as your late 20s.

Enzyme Decline

Alcohol is primarily metabolized in the liver by two key enzymes: alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) and aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH). As we age, the liver naturally produces less of these enzymes. This means the toxic byproduct of alcohol—acetaldehyde—remains in your bloodstream longer, causing the signature “sick” feeling to linger. [3]

Lower Body Water Composition

Your body’s water content naturally drops as you age. Because alcohol is water-soluble, having less “solvent” in your system means the alcohol becomes more concentrated in your blood. Consequently, your Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) rises faster and hits harder than it did a decade ago. [4]

Slower Cellular Repair

Just as muscle recovery takes longer after an intense HIIT session, your cells take longer to bounce back from the inflammatory “hit” of alcohol. Research confirms that aging organs are more sensitive to alcohol’s toxicity, extending the recovery window from a single day to nearly three. [5]

The Sleep Factor

Alcohol significantly disrupts REM sleep—the stage associated with cognitive clarity and emotional regulation. Studies show that this disruption is more severe as we age, leading to the intense “brain fog” and fatigue that define a modern-day hangover.
