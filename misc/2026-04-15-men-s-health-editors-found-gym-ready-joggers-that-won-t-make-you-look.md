What to Consider

Materials

The perfect jogger has to start with soft material—comfort is king here and that should never change. Go for breathable fabrics, either natural like cotton or bamboo, or smart fabrics that are synthetic but have dry-fit and thermo-control tech implemented in them, like some fleece and other hi-tech fibers. We also recommend keeping an eye out for fabrics with stretch (think Lycra, spandex, or elastane) and any other features that promote movement. If you’re looking to work out in joggers, look for nylon and Lycra for stretch, flexibility, and quick-drying properties.

Fit

Joggers come in a variety of fits to suit different preferences and occasions. Finding a fit that sits close to the body throughout the hip and thigh and tapers slightly towards the cuff will give you the most versatility of wear. Here’s a breakdown of the most common types.

Slim Fit

This fit is tapered from the thighs down to the ankles, offering a sleek, close-to-the-body look. If you’re looking to achieve a modern, stylish vibe that works for casual outings, light workouts, or even travel—this is it. You can wear it with fitted t-shirts, hoodies, or casual sneakers for a polished yet comfortable outfit.

Relaxed Fit

A relaxed fit is roomier through the thighs and hips with a less restrictive feel, while still tapering slightly at the ankles. It’s perfect for lounging, light activities, or anyone who prefers extra comfort without a tight fit. Pair it with oversized sweatshirts, loose tees, or slip-on shoes for a cozy, laid-back style.

Regular Fit

A middle ground between slim and relaxed—fitted enough to look sharp but not tight. We recommend this fit for everyday wear and versatile styling. From crewnecks to polos, or even casual button-downs for a balanced, casual look.

Tapered Fit

Tapered fits start with more room around the thighs and gradually narrow down to the ankles. They’re best for activities where you need mobility and a streamlined silhouette, like gym sessions or errands. In terms of styling, wear them with athletic tops, running shoes, or casual sneakers.

Waistband

A comfortable waistband can make or break your joggers. Choose joggers that have an elastic waistband with adjustable drawstrings for a custom fit. Look for a smooth, non-restrictive fit that stays secure without digging in.

Pockets

Features like hidden zippered pockets enhance functionality, especially for travel, and contribute to both the practicality and style of the joggers. Think about what you typically carry—phone, wallet, keys, etc.—and how much storage you'll need for them on your person.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Are Joggers?

Joggers are basically upgraded sweatpants. They're the comfier cousin of jeans and chinos, but way more stylish than the baggy sweats you might lounge around in at home. Picture this: They're made with soft, stretchy materials, have a slim or tapered fit, and often come with an elastic waistband and cuffs at the ankle. They're perfect for looking put-together without sacrificing comfort, whether you're hitting the gym, running errands, or just kicking back on the couch.

Are Joggers Acceptable at Work?

It depends on where you work. If you're in a casual or creative office, joggers can totally work—as long as they don't scream "gym clothes." Go for a clean, tailored fabric (think twill, chinos, or tech materials) like Lululemon's ABC Jogger. Throw on a flannel, layered over a t-shirt, paired with sneakers. Or even a sleek sweater, and you're good to go. But if your office vibe is more suits-and-ties, joggers might not fly. Always read the room (or the dress code).

What Body Types Look Good in Joggers?

Joggers narrow your body's natural silhouette because the legs taper to an elastic ankle band. This doesn't mean that joggers are only suitable for slim people. Since joggers are meant to fit loosely around your hips and thighs, you can find a pair to suit any body type. The key is to find a pair that fits comfortably from the knee up and doesn't get too tight as it tapers from the knee to the ankle. Joggers should not fit like leggings or compression pants on anyone, so if you try on a pair and they're tight all the way through the leg, they're not for you regardless of how slim you are.

What's the Differences Between Sweatpants and Joggers?

Sweatpants tend to have a looser fit throughout the leg, while joggers feature a more tailored or tapered fit. This means the legs of joggers will often touch your skin and feel a bit tight if you’ve got beefy calves.

Traditional sweatpants are often made from thicker materials like fleece and have a casual appearance, while joggers can be made from a wider range of materials, including activewear fabrics like nylon or polyester.

Ankle

Joggers have ribbed cuffs at the ankles and a sportier appearance compared to the relaxed vibe of traditional sweatpants, whereas sweatpants can have either an open design or ribbed cuffs.

Style

Sweatpants have a more relaxed and traditional look, while joggers are designed with a sporty aesthetic, often featuring modern details like zipper pockets and paneling. And because of the difference in ankle cuffs and fit, sweatpants look more casual, whereas joggers look more elevated and sleek.

Function

Joggers are versatile for both athletic and casual wear, while sweatpants are primarily associated with casual lounging and relaxation.

Occasion

Sweatpants are ideal for cozy indoor activities, while joggers can transition from the gym to casual outings due to their sleeker appearance.
