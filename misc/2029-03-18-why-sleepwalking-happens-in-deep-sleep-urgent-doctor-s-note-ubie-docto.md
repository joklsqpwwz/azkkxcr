Sleepwalking most often occurs during deep, non-REM sleep — typically in the first third of the night. During this stage, the brain is in a state of reduced consciousness, which allows partial arousal to trigger complex behaviors like walking, talking, or performing routine tasks while a person remains asleep and unaware.

Because sleepwalking can stem from many underlying causes — including stress, sleep deprivation, medications, or other sleep disorders — understanding what's driving your episodes is the first step toward better rest and safety.
