Understanding Psoralens: Why Celery Juice Might Increase Sun Sensitivity

Raw celery juice has become a wellness trend, praised for its vitamins, minerals and potential digestive benefits. Yet, like any concentrated plant extract, it comes with side effects. One lesser-known risk involves natural compounds called psoralens, which can make skin more sensitive to sunlight. Here's what you need to know.

What Are Psoralens?

Psoralens (a type of furanocoumarin) are chemicals found in celery, parsley, parsnip, figs and other plants.

They serve as a natural defense against pests.

When ingested or applied to the skin, psoralens can interact with UV-A rays (320–400 nm).

Studies in photobiology journals show that psoralens intercalate into DNA strands. Upon UV-A exposure, they form cross-links that trigger skin cell damage, inflammation and the telltale redness of phototoxicity.

How Psoralens Increase Sun Sensitivity

Psoralen ingestion or topical contact deposits these chemicals in the skin.

UV-A rays activate the psoralens, producing reactive oxygen species (free radicals).

Free radicals damage cellular structures—cell membranes, proteins and DNA.

Sunburn-like redness and pain

Swelling or blistering in severe cases

Long-term risk of hyperpigmentation or accelerated photoaging

Unlike a typical sunburn (caused by UV-B), psoralen-induced phototoxicity can occur even on cloudy days or through window glass.

Raw Celery Juice Side Effects

Besides photosensitivity, concentrated raw celery juice may cause:

Digestive upset (bloating or diarrhea) from high fiber and salt content

Allergic reactions in celery-sensitive individuals (hives, itching)

Electrolyte imbalance (high potassium levels) in those with kidney issues

Headaches or dizziness, possibly from sudden fluid shifts

Photosensitivity is often overlooked. If you're sipping large amounts of raw celery juice daily, you could unknowingly heighten your risk of sun-induced skin injury.

Who's Most at Risk?

Not everyone reacts the same way. Risk factors include:

High celery juice intake (more psoralens)

Fair skin or a history of sunburns

Medications or supplements that also increase photosensitivity (certain antibiotics, retinoids)

Prolonged or intense sun exposure without adequate protection

Even if your skin isn't extremely fair, combining psoralens with tanning or outdoor sports can trigger an unpleasant reaction.

Minimizing Your Risk

You don't have to give up raw celery juice entirely, but consider these steps:

Drink in moderation: limit intake to 4–8 ounces per day rather than large volumes.

Time your consumption: avoid celery juice right before or during peak sun hours (10 a.m.–4 p.m.).

Use broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) on exposed skin, reapplying every two hours.
