What Is Colchicine Prescribed For?

Colchicine is a prescription anti-inflammatory medication that’s been used safely for decades (generic Lodoco). Traditionally used to treat gout and inflammatory conditions, it can support cardiovascular health by calming chronic, low-grade inflammation within blood vessels.

Inflammation inside blood vessels can quietly damage arteries over time and raise heart risk, even in people whose cholesterol numbers look normal.

Instead of waiting for symptoms or advanced plaque to develop, colchicine targets one of the underlying drivers of heart disease: ongoing immune activity in the blood vessels that fuels atherosclerosis, a condition in which arteries slowly become damaged and narrowed, making it harder for blood to flow freely to the heart and brain.

How Does Colchicine Work?

Colchicine support cardiovascular health by calming an overactive immune response inside blood vessels. By reducing inflammatory signals that activate white blood cells within the vessel walls, colchicine supports the health of artery walls and decreases the risk of serious events like heart attacks and strokes.

Unlike cholesterol-lowering medications, colchicine does not affect lipid levels. Instead, it targets inflammation itself, addressing another driver of cardiovascular risk.

Because it works through a different pathway, colchicine can complement other heart-health therapies as part of a comprehensive prevention strategy.

Who Is Colchicine Right For?

Colchicine may be appropriate for people who are working to reduce long-term cardiovascular risk and address underlying inflammation.

It’s often considered for individuals who:

Have higher heart risk even though their cholesterol levels look “normal”

Show signs of body-wide inflammation or inflamed blood vessels

Want a proactive, preventive approach to heart health

Colchicine is not a replacement for lifestyle changes or lipid management. It’s a targeted tool to address inflammation as part of a broader heart health plan.

Contraindications

Colchicine may not be appropriate if you:

Have severe kidney or liver disease

Have a known hypersensitivity to colchicine

Take certain medications that interact with colchicine

Have active digestive conditions that could affect how well you tolerate it

Have certain blood disorders or very low blood cell counts

Final eligibility is determined by a licensed healthcare provider.

Possible Side Effects

Common or potential side effects may include:

Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain)

Fatigue

Headache

Muscle pain or weakness (rare, higher risk with drug interactions)

Changes in blood counts (rare)

Most side effects improve with dose adjustment or discontinuation.

Warnings

Colchicine may interact with certain medications, including statins, antifungals, and antibiotics.

Patients should tell their doctor about all medications and supplements they’re taking before starting therapy.

Dose adjustments may be required for kidney or liver impairment.
