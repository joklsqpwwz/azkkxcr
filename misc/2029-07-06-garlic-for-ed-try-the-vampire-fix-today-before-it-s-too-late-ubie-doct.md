Can garlic help with erectile dysfunction? Research suggests garlic—especially aged garlic extract (AGE)—may modestly improve ED by enhancing blood vessel function (endothelial health) and lowering blood pressure, two key factors in achieving and maintaining erections. The strongest evidence supports using AGE alongside standard ED treatments rather than as a standalone cure.

Typical dosing is 1,200–2,400 mg per day of aged garlic extract or 1–2 crushed raw cloves, with noticeable benefits usually taking 8–12 weeks. Risks include stomach upset, body odor, and increased bleeding—especially if you take blood thinners, blood pressure medications, or diabetes drugs. Certain people should avoid garlic supplementation entirely, and persistent ED may signal an underlying condition like heart disease or diabetes.

Scientific Evidence

1. Endothelial Function Improvement

Budoff MJ et al. (2010). Br J Nutr. PMID: 20483971

Population: Men with high cholesterol (no ED requirement).

Intervention: 2,400 mg/day aged garlic extract vs. placebo for 12 weeks.

Outcome: Significant improvement in brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (a measure of endothelial health).

Implication: Better endothelial function may translate to improved penile blood flow.

2. Blood Pressure Reduction

Ried K, Frank OR, Stocks NP. (2008). BMC Cardiovasc Disord. PMID: 18776798

Review of 39 trials, 2,400 mg garlic powder or 900 mg AGE daily for 2–6 months.

3. Indirect Benefits on Sexual Health

While direct trials of garlic for ED are limited, improvements in BP and endothelial function support its potential role. Reduced arterial stiffness and enhanced NO bioavailability are central to achieving and maintaining erections.

Using Garlic Safely for ED

Recommended Forms & Dosages

Aged Garlic Extract:• 600–1,200 mg twice daily (total 1,200–2,400 mg/day), standardized to S-allyl cysteine.

Raw Garlic Cloves:• 1–2 cloves (3–6 g) crushed and consumed daily, ideally on an empty stomach.

Potential Side Effects

Gastrointestinal upset (gas, heartburn)

Garlic breath and body odor (minimal with AGE)

Increased bleeding risk—caution if on anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin)

Allergic reactions (rare)

Drug Interactions & Precautions

Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets: May enhance bleeding. Monitor closely.

Antihypertensives: May potentiate BP-lowering effect; monitor blood pressure.

Diabetes Medications: Garlic can lower blood sugar; watch for hypoglycemia.

Always inform your healthcare provider before starting long-term garlic supplements, especially if you take prescription drugs.
