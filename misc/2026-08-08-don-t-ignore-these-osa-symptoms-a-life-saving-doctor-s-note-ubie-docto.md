What are the main symptoms of Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a common sleep disorder marked by three key symptoms:

Loud, chronic snoring — often noticed by a bed partner

Frequent nighttime awakenings — sometimes with gasping or choking

Excessive daytime sleepiness — feeling tired even after a full night's sleep

Other signs may include morning headaches, difficulty concentrating, and irritability. Left untreated, OSA can raise your risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke, so recognizing symptoms early matters.

Explanation

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a condition where a person stops breathing for short periods during sleep. This happens because the throat muscles relax too much and block the airway.

Common Symptoms:

Loud Snoring: This is often the most noticeable sign and can be very disruptive to others.

Frequent Awakenings: People with OSA may wake up many times during the night, often without realizing it.

Daytime Sleepiness: Because sleep is interrupted, people often feel very tired during the day.

Other symptoms can include waking up with a dry mouth or sore throat, morning headaches, and trouble concentrating during the day. Some people might also experience mood changes, such as feeling more irritable.

These symptoms can affect daily life, making it hard to stay awake and alert.
