# How Technology Helped This Guy Manage His Diabetes

WHEN JOSHUA VERGARA, 35, found out he had type 2 diabetes in 2014, the diagnosis explained why he was always so tired. The drag was his body screaming, “This isn’t the way I need to live!” Vergara says. To change, the tech content creator got himself a Dexcom continuous glucose monitor (CGM). Its real-time blood-sugar-level feedback “is a constant reminder of where I am, where I want to be, and where I want to go,” he says. And the monitor kick-started a whole new way to live:

THROUGH HIS CGM'S super-personalized feedback, Vergara learned “how my body is reacting to life.” So if he eats his favorite meal of Korean barbecue and rice, he watches his blood sugar tick up—until a post-meal walk (or round of squats) helps bring back balance.

Move It

VERGARA CAME TO realize that you don’t have to be athletic to be active. Frequent travel makes a gym routine difficult, so he commits to moving every day, no matter what that looks like: jujitsu, lifting weights, hitting his 10,000-step goal. “Most days, I just make sure to get my steps in. A net positive is better than zero.”

Step Back

HIS CMG DATA filters onto his phone’s home screen for tracking ease—but the constant data feedback can be overwhelming, he says. “It’s easy to see a high number and start to beat myself up.”

Tough, since stress can boost levels, too. Vergara reminds himself that “the data is guidance. A high number after lunch might make me more mindful of my dinner and my activity levels leading up to it.” And the CGM keeps him honest about his big-picture health.

Blood Sugar, Made Easier
