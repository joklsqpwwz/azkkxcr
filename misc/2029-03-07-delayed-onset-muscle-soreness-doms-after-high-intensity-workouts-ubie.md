Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS) After High-Intensity Workouts

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Muscle soreness after intense or unfamiliar workouts is called Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS). DOMS typically starts 12 to 24 hours after exercise, peaks between 24 and 72 hours, and resolves within about a week as microtears heal and inflammation subsides.

Severity depends on several factors, including eccentric loading, workout volume, hydration, nutrition, rest, and how gradually you progress your training. Below, you'll find detailed timelines, risk factors, management strategies, prevention tips, and warning signs to help guide your recovery.

Because muscle soreness can sometimes overlap with symptoms of injury, dehydration, or other underlying conditions, it's important to know when soreness is normal and when it warrants attention. If you've just jumped into CrossFit or any other high-intensity training program, you may be wondering why you're feeling stiff, achy, or tender a day or two after your workout. This common experience is known as Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS). Understanding DOMS can help you manage discomfort, optimize recovery, and stay on track with your fitness goals.

What Is DOMS?

DOMS is muscle pain or stiffness that appears 12–24 hours after unaccustomed or intense exercise.

It peaks around 24–72 hours post-workout and usually eases up within 5–7 days.

Microscopic damage to muscle fibers (microtears) and the resulting inflammation cause the discomfort.

Soreness after starting CrossFit is often more noticeable because CrossFit workouts combine heavy loads, fast movements, and lots of eccentric (lengthening) muscle actions—all prime triggers for DOMS.

Why DOMS Happens

Microtrauma: High-intensity or novel movements create tiny tears in muscle fibers.

Inflammation: The body's repair process increases blood flow and sends immune cells to the damaged area, producing soreness and stiffness.

Repair & Adaptation: Muscles rebuild stronger to adapt to the new demands, which is the key to gains in strength and endurance.

Typical Timeline

0–12 hours post-workout: You might feel normal or mildly tight.

12–24 hours: Initial onset of stiffness or tenderness.

24–72 hours: Peak soreness—difficulty squatting, lunging, or lifting without discomfort.

4–7 days: Gradual easing of pain and return to baseline.

Common Symptoms

Tenderness when touching or pressing the muscles

Stiffness, especially after periods of rest

Reduced range of motion (e.g., squatting deeper than feels comfortable)

Mild swelling or warmth around the sore area

Risk Factors

Starting a new fitness program or trying unfamiliar movements

Performing high volumes of eccentric (lengthening) exercises, such as negative pull-ups or downhill lunges

Lifting heavier loads than usual without proper progression

Skipping warm-ups or cooldowns

Exercising when fatigued or dehydrated

Is This Normal?

Yes. Soreness after starting CrossFit—or any new, intense routine—is a normal part of the body's adaptation process. It shows you challenged your muscles. However, normal DOMS should improve day by day. If pain worsens or doesn't ease within a week, consider adjusting your approach or seeking professional advice.

Managing DOMS

While you can't eliminate DOMS entirely, you can ease the discomfort:

Active Recovery• Light cycling, walking, or swimming to boost circulation• Gentle mobility drills and dynamic stretches

Hydration & Nutrition• Drink plenty of water to support recovery processes• Eat protein-rich foods (lean meats, dairy, legumes) to aid muscle repair

Rest & Sleep• Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep per night• Take easy days or rest days between intense sessions

Topical Relief• Over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen or acetaminophen) if needed• Ice packs or heat pads applied for 10–15 minutes at a time

Self-Massage & Foam Rolling• Roll gently over sore areas for 30–60 seconds• Use a lacrosse ball on tight spots (e.g., glutes, calves)

Dynamic Warm-Up: Before each session, spend 5–10 minutes on bodyweight movements (leg swings, arm circles, lunges) to prepare muscles.

Cooldown Routine: Finish workouts with 5–10 minutes of light cardio plus static stretches targeting the day's major muscle groups.

Yoga or Mobility Classes: Incorporate a weekly class focused on flexibility and controlled movements.

Contrast Baths: Alternate between warm and cold water immersion to stimulate circulation.

Preventing Excessive Soreness

Progress Gradually: Increase intensity, volume, or load by no more than 10% per week

Prioritize Technique: Focus on form before adding weight or speed

Alternate Muscle Groups: Don't train the same muscle groups heavily on consecutive days

Listen to Your Body: If you feel sharp pain or extreme fatigue, back off or modify the workout

Stay Consistent: Regular training conditions your muscles to handle stress better over time

When It's More Than DOMS

Sometimes, muscle pain signals a more serious issue. Seek immediate medical attention or speak to a doctor if you experience:

Severe, crippling pain that doesn't improve with rest

Swelling, redness, or heat that worsens over time

Muscle weakness so profound you can't move the limb

Dark, tea-colored urine (possible rhabdomyolysis)

Fever, chills, or signs of infection
