Understanding Skin pH

What is skin pH?The pH scale ranges from 0 (very acidic) to 14 (very alkaline). Healthy skin typically has a slightly acidic pH between 4.5 and 5.5.

A balanced acid mantle protects against bacteria, fungi, and environmental pollutants.

Disrupted pH can lead to dryness, sensitivity, acne, and premature aging.

The Science Behind ACV and Skin pH

Acidity of ACV

Raw, unfiltered ACV has a pH around 2.5 due to acetic acid and other organic acids.

When diluted appropriately, ACV can help restore the skin's natural acidity.

Antimicrobial properties

Acetic acid inhibits growth of certain acne-causing bacteria (e.g., Propionibacterium acnes).

It may help reduce mild fungal issues like dandruff or yeast-related rashes.

Supporting studies

A 2014 study in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology found that low-pH products can reinforce the skin barrier and reduce irritation.

How to Use ACV Safely

1. Choose the right product

Opt for raw, unfiltered, organic apple cider vinegar with "the mother" for maximum nutrients.

Avoid brands with added sugars or artificial ingredients.

2. Dilution ratios

For normal to oily skin:• Mix 1 part ACV to 3–4 parts water.

For sensitive or dry skin:• Mix 1 part ACV to 6–8 parts water.

Always prepare a fresh batch; do not store diluted ACV longer than 48 hours.

3. Patch test

Before applying to your face:

Choose a small area (e.g., inner forearm).

Apply a pea-sized drop of diluted ACV and wait 24 hours.

If no redness, burning, or itching occurs, it's generally safe to proceed.

4. Application steps

Cleanse face with a gentle, pH-balanced cleanser.

Apply diluted ACV with a cotton pad or ball, sweeping across the face—avoid the eye area.

[ACV for Clear](https://benpepsi.com/)

Leave on for 5–10 minutes (or less if you're new to ACV).

Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water.

Moisturize with a non-comedogenic, fragrance-free cream or lotion.

Frequency: Start 2–3 times per week, then adjust based on skin tolerance.

Who Should Be Cautious

Sensitive skin or conditions (eczema, rosacea): Perform extra-mild dilutions (1:8–1:10) and limit to once weekly.

Open wounds or sunburn: Avoid ACV—acid may sting or slow healing.

Allergic history: If you've reacted to other acidic skincare ingredients, skip ACV.

Possible Side Effects

Irritation or burning: Often due to undiluted or overly concentrated ACV.

Dryness or flakiness: Can occur if used too frequently.

Long-term barrier damage: Overuse may weaken the acid mantle rather than strengthen it.
