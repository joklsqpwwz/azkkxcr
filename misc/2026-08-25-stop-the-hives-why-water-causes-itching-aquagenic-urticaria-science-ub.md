Why Water Touching Skin Causes Hives and Itching: Aquagenic Urticaria Science

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Aquagenic urticaria is a rare form of chronic hives triggered by water exposure, regardless of temperature or source. Contact with water can cause immediate welts, intense itching, or burning sensations, likely due to immune-mediated histamine release, osmotic shifts across the skin, or reactions to trace minerals. Common triggers include showering, sweating, rain, and even tears. Treatment options include high-dose non-sedating antihistamines, topical barrier creams (like petrolatum), phototherapy, and biologics such as omalizumab for severe cases.

Diagnosis typically involves a water challenge test, and management requires identifying personal triggers and severity. Because symptoms can mimic other conditions like cholinergic or cold-induced urticaria, proper evaluation is essential.

What Is Aquagenic Urticaria?

A subtype of chronic urticaria ("hives") triggered specifically by contact with water—regardless of temperature or source.

Presents as small, red or skin-colored welts (wheals) surrounded by itching or burning.

Symptoms typically appear within minutes of water exposure and subside within 30–60 minutes once the skin dries.

Common Signs and Symptoms

When water touching skin causes hives itch, you may notice:

Tiny, raised bumps (1–3 mm diameter) or larger patches of welts.

Intense itching, stinging, or burning sensations.

Clear separation between affected and unaffected skin.

Possible mild swelling (angioedema) around lips or eyes if water contacts the face.

Effective Treatment Strategies

While there's no cure, most people can control aquagenic urticaria with these approaches:

Second-generation H1 blockers (cetirizine, loratadine) are first-line.

Higher doses than standard allergy regimens may be required—always under medical guidance.

Petrolatum or zinc oxide creams applied 20 minutes before water exposure can reduce direct contact.

Emollients to support skin barrier function.

Practical Tips to Minimize Flare-Ups

Test water temperature: lukewarm is less irritating than extreme hot or cold.

Avoid heavy fabrics or wool immediately after water contact to reduce friction.

Keep skin well-moisturized; dry, cracked skin may worsen reactions.

If swimming in chlorinated pools or seawater, rinse off thoroughly and reapply barrier cream.

What to Watch For: When to Seek Immediate Care

Most reactions remain localized, but occasionally they can escalate:

Rapid swelling of lips, tongue, or throat (angioedema).

Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or tightness in the chest.

Lightheadedness, dizziness, or fainting.

Signs of secondary infection in scratched areas (increased redness, warmth, pus).

If you experience any of these, seek emergency medical help without delay. Always speak to a doctor if you're uncertain whether symptoms could be life threatening or serious.
