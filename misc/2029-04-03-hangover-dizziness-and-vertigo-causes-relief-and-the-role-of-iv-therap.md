Introduction

Few hangover symptoms are as debilitating as dizziness and vertigo. Beyond the headache and nausea, the sensation of the room spinning or feeling unsteady can make even simple tasks dangerous. Understanding why these symptoms occur and how IV therapy can help provide a pathway to faster recovery.

Hangover-Related Dizziness and Vertigo

Alcohol disrupts multiple systems in the body, leading to dizziness and vertigo:

Dehydration: Alcohol is a diuretic, increasing urine output and fluid loss. Dehydration reduces blood volume, lowering blood pressure and causing lightheadedness.

[Dizziness and Vertigo Causes](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/blog/3852497381.html)

Electrolyte Imbalance: Excess urination flushes out sodium, potassium, and magnesium, which are essential for nerve and muscle function. Imbalances can trigger vertigo and weakness.

Inner Ear Effects: Alcohol alters fluid balance in the inner ear, disturbing equilibrium and contributing to vertigo.

Inflammation and Acetaldehyde: Alcohol metabolism produces acetaldehyde, a toxic byproduct that increases inflammation and worsens dizziness.

According to the Cleveland Clinic, dizziness and vertigo are hallmark hangover symptoms, often lasting up to 24 hours depending on alcohol intake.

Evidence-Based Benefits & Context

Hydration and Vertigo Relief

Rehydration restores blood volume and stabilizes blood pressure, reducing dizziness. IV fluids are absorbed immediately, unlike oral hydration which can be slowed by nausea.

Electrolytes and Balance

Electrolytes regulate nerve signaling and muscle contractions. Restoring sodium and potassium helps normalize inner ear function, reducing vertigo.

NAD⁺ and Cellular Recovery

NAD⁺ supports mitochondrial energy production and liver detoxification. Supplementation may accelerate alcohol metabolism and reduce fatigue.

B Vitamins and Neurological Support

Alcohol depletes B vitamins, especially thiamine (B1), which is critical for brain and nerve function. IV replenishment reduces neurological symptoms like dizziness and confusion.
