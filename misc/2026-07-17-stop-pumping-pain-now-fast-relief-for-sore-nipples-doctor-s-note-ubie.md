What can I do to reduce nipple soreness caused by pumping?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Nipple soreness from pumping is often caused by improper flange fit, excessive suction, or inadequate pump hygiene. To reduce discomfort, ensure your breast pump flange is correctly sized for your nipple, adjust suction to the lowest effective level, and clean pump parts thoroughly after each use to prevent bacterial irritation. Applying lanolin cream or expressed milk can also promote healing.

Because nipple soreness can sometimes signal underlying issues like thrush, mastitis, or improper latch technique, it's worth exploring your symptoms in more detail. Explanation

Nipple soreness from pumping can be uncomfortable, but there are ways to help reduce it. First, make sure your breast pump fits correctly. A poor fit can cause unnecessary friction and pain. Check that the flange, the part that goes over your nipple, is the right size. If it's too small or too big, it can lead to soreness.

Another important tip is to adjust the suction settings on your pump. Start with a lower suction level and gradually increase it to a comfortable level. High suction can cause pain and irritation, so it's important to find a setting that feels right for you.
