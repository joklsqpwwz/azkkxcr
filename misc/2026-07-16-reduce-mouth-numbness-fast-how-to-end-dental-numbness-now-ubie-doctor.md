Numbness in your mouth after a dental procedure typically fades within 2–4 hours, but you can speed up recovery. The most effective option is OraVerse (phentolamine mesylate), a prescription medication your dentist can inject to reverse dental anesthesia in about half the usual time. Other helpful methods include gentle facial massage, light physical activity to boost circulation, and applying a warm compress to the area.

If numbness lasts longer than expected or feels unusual, it may signal something beyond typical anesthesia wear-off—such as nerve irritation or an underlying condition. Explanation

After a dental procedure, your mouth might feel numb for a while due to the anesthesia used. One way to help reduce this numbness is by using a medication called OraVerse. OraVerse is designed to speed up the return to normal sensation in your mouth by helping the effects of the local anesthetic wear off more quickly.

Here are some key points about OraVerse:

Purpose: It is used to reverse the numbness caused by local anesthetics in dental procedures.

How it works: OraVerse works by increasing blood flow in the area where anesthesia was applied, which helps the anesthetic clear out faster.

Usage: It is typically administered by the dentist after the procedure is complete.
