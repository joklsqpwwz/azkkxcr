PRP or Rogaine? Comparing the Best Hair Growth Treatments

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

PRP therapy and minoxidil (Rogaine) are two proven treatments for hair regrowth, each with distinct advantages. PRP uses periodic in-office injections of growth factors, often producing thicker hair within 3 to 6 months at a higher cost. Minoxidil, an FDA-approved daily topical vasodilator, delivers modest regrowth over 4 to 12 months at a lower cost.

Choosing between them depends on your budget, treatment frequency, side effect tolerance, and comfort with needles. Efficacy, safety, and candidacy also vary by individual—making a personalized assessment essential before starting either option.

PRP vs Minoxidil: Comparing the Best Hair Growth Treatments

Hair thinning and loss affect millions of people worldwide. Two of the most popular treatments today are platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy and minoxidil (commonly known by the brand name Rogaine). Understanding how each works, their effectiveness, side effects and costs can help you decide which option best fits your lifestyle and goals.

What Causes Hair Loss?

Before diving into PRP vs Minoxidil, it helps to know why hair falls out. Common causes include:

Genetics (androgenetic alopecia)

Hormonal changes (thyroid, pregnancy, menopause)

Stress or illness

Nutritional deficiencies

Scalp conditions (psoriasis, dermatitis)

If you're experiencing unexpected hair loss alongside other symptoms like fatigue, weight changes, or skin issues, take this free symptom assessment to better understand what might be causing your hair loss before consulting with a healthcare provider.

Understanding PRP and Minoxidil

What Is PRP Therapy?

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses your own blood platelets—rich in growth factors—to stimulate hair follicles. A typical PRP session involves:

Drawing a small blood sample

Spinning it in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets

Injecting the PRP into targeted areas of the scalp

PRP aims to revitalize dormant follicles, improve blood supply, and encourage new hair growth.

What Is Minoxidil?

Minoxidil is an FDA-approved topical solution available over the counter. It's applied directly to the scalp, usually twice daily:

2% and 5% concentrations for men and women

Foam or liquid formulations

Minoxidil widens blood vessels around hair follicles, prolonging the growth phase and increasing follicle size.

Efficacy and Evidence

Credible studies and clinical trials suggest:

PRP can improve hair density by 20–40% after 6 months (Journal of Cutaneous and Aesthetic Surgery)

Minoxidil users often experience 30–40% reduction in hair loss and modest regrowth over 12 months (American Academy of Dermatology)

Head-to-head comparisons are limited, but many dermatologists combine both for synergistic effects.

Side Effects and Risks

PRP Side Effects

Mild pain or swelling at injection sites

Temporary bruising or bleeding

Infection risk (very low if sterile technique followed)

Minoxidil Side Effects

Scalp itching, redness or flaking

Unwanted facial hair in rare cases

Initial shedding phase (temporary)

Who Is a Good Candidate?

PRP may be best if you:

Prefer infrequent in-office treatments

Don't mind injections

Have early to moderate thinning

Minoxidil may be best if you:

Want a cost-effective, at-home solution

Don't have needle phobia

Are willing to apply product daily

In some cases, combining PRP and Minoxidil offers improved outcomes. Always discuss your medical history, medications, and expectations with a qualified provider.

Realistic Expectations

Both treatments take months to show visible improvement.

Complete reversal of advanced baldness is unlikely.

Maintenance is key—stopping either treatment may halt progress.
