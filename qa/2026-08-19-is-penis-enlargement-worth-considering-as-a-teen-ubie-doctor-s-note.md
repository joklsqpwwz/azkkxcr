# Is penis enlargement worth considering as a teen? | Ubie Doctor's Note

Is penis enlargement worth considering as a teen?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Penis enlargement is generally not recommended for teenagers. Most teens are still developing into their early 20s, fall within normal size ranges for their age, and there are no safe or proven enlargement methods approved for minors. Many advertised options—including pumps, extenders, creams, and surgery—carry real risks such as tissue injury, reduced sensitivity, scarring, and emotional distress.

Key factors to understand include normal adolescent development, common myths about size, the limited evidence and documented risks of enlargement products, mental health considerations like body image concerns, and when to consult a healthcare professional.

1. Normal Development During Teen Years

Puberty timeline varies: Most boys start puberty between ages 9 and 14. Penis growth often continues into the early 20s.

Average size: Studies show average erect penis length around 5–6 inches (12.5–15.5 cm). Girth (circumference) averages about 4.5–5 inches (11.5–12.7 cm).

Growth patterns: Growth spurts usually happen over several years. Some teens grow earlier, others later—both are normal.

3. Physical and Psychological Risks

Tissue damage: Overstretching or excessive suction can tear blood vessels and tissues, leading to scarring.

Erectile issues: Nerve or vascular injury may impair future erections.

Emotional distress: Failed attempts or complications can worsen body image, leading to anxiety or depression.

Financial cost: Many products are expensive, unregulated, and ultimately ineffective.
